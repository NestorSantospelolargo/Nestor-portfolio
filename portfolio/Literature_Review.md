# Current AI Literature Review

## Search Algorithms Assignment

The search assignment was connected to the course literature on classical artificial intelligence, especially the textbook material from *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* by Russell and Norvig. This source provided the theoretical foundation for understanding search as a method for solving problems by exploring possible states and actions.

Through this assignment, I reviewed concepts related to uninformed and informed search, state spaces, goal tests, path costs, and heuristics. These ideas helped me understand how AI systems can solve problems systematically rather than relying on direct procedural programming.

This assignment connected directly to the course topics of solving problems by searching and intelligent agents. It also helped me understand why search is one of the foundational techniques in artificial intelligence.

## Intelligent Agents Assignment

The intelligent agents assignment was also strongly connected to Russell and Norvig’s treatment of AI as the study of rational agents. The course syllabus describes artificial intelligence from the perspective of designing computational agents that act optimally with limited information and computational resources. This idea guided my understanding of agents as systems that perceive an environment, make decisions, and take actions.

In this assignment, I reviewed concepts such as agent types, environments, percepts, actions, rationality, and performance measures. These concepts helped me understand that intelligence can be modeled as decision-making behavior within an environment.

## Local Search Algorithms Assignment

The local search assignment connected to literature on optimization and beyond-classical search. Russell and Norvig explain local search as a method for solving problems where the path to the solution is less important than the final state. This was useful for understanding why algorithms such as hill climbing, simulated annealing, and genetic algorithms are used in optimization problems.

This assignment helped me review how AI systems can search for good solutions without exhaustively exploring the entire state space. Instead of maintaining complete paths, local search methods improve candidate solutions over time.

The literature behind this assignment helped me understand the tradeoff between efficiency and completeness. Local search methods can be powerful in large search spaces, but they may also be affected by local maxima, randomness, or sensitivity to initial conditions.

## Adversarial Search Assignment

The adversarial search assignment was connected to the literature on game-playing AI, especially concepts such as minimax search and alpha-beta pruning. These methods are important because they model decision-making in competitive environments where another agent is also making strategic choices.

This assignment helped me understand how AI systems evaluate possible future outcomes and choose actions based on an opponent’s expected behavior. Unlike simple search problems, adversarial search requires reasoning about another agent’s goals and possible moves.

The literature reviewed for this assignment connected to classical AI because adversarial search shows how reasoning, planning, and evaluation functions can be used to make intelligent decisions in competitive situations.

## Constraint Satisfaction Problem: Zebra Problem

The Zebra Problem assignment was based on Constraint Satisfaction Problem literature, especially the CSP material from Russell and Norvig. The report describes CSPs as problems involving variables, domains, and constraints, where the goal is to find an assignment that satisfies all restrictions. The assignment also cited *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* as one of its references.

In this assignment, I reviewed different ways to represent the Zebra Problem as a CSP, including house-based, attribute-position, and binary constraint representations. The attribute-position representation was preferred because it was compact, clear, and efficient for constraint propagation.

This assignment helped me understand how classical AI methods can solve structured reasoning problems. Instead of guessing, the CSP approach uses constraints to eliminate impossible solutions and arrive at a valid result. In the final solution, the Japanese owns the zebra, and the Norwegian drinks water.

## Python vs. NumPy Performance Assignment

The Python versus NumPy assignment involved reviewing sources related to Python performance, NumPy, vectorization, and data visualization. The report cited the Python Software Foundation, Intel Granulate, NumPy documentation, Practical Data Science, Matplotlib documentation, and OpenAI.

The literature explained that Python is flexible and readable but can be slow for large numerical computations because it is interpreted and dynamically typed. NumPy addresses this limitation by using optimized low-level code and vectorized operations. The report specifically described NumPy as useful for complex mathematical operations because it avoids much of the overhead of pure Python loops.

This assignment connected to AI because performance matters when implementing computationally intensive algorithms. In machine learning, search, optimization, and numerical computation, choosing efficient tools can greatly affect scalability. The results showed that NumPy was significantly faster than pure Python, especially for large matrix operations.

## Is Artificial Intelligence True Intelligence? Debate Essay

This essay required reviewing philosophical and scientific literature related to intelligence, cognition, consciousness, and artificial intelligence. The project examined both sides of the debate regarding whether AI should be considered genuine intelligence.

The literature reviewed included functionalism, Bandura's Social Learning Theory, the AI Effect, Searle's Chinese Room argument, Chalmers' work on consciousness, and Dennett's theory of intentionality. These sources provided different perspectives on how intelligence should be defined and whether artificial systems satisfy those definitions.

One of the central aspects of this assignment is that the answer depends heavily on how intelligence is defined. Functionalist perspectives emphasize behavior, learning, and problem-solving, while other perspectives focus on understanding, consciousness, meaning, and subjective experience.

This essay is relevant in the course when studying Large Language Models. Many of the questions raised in the debate directly relate to current discussions about whether LLMs truly reason, understand language, or merely predict patterns based on training data. The assignment helped me approach these questions more critically and recognize that intelligence is a more complex concept than I originally assumed.

## Artemis II Agent Architecture Assignment

The Artemis II report used sources related to NASA’s Artemis II mission, the Orion Guidance, Navigation, and Control system, space mission analysis, autonomous systems, and Russell and Norvig’s AI textbook.

This assignment reviewed Artemis II as a hierarchical multi-agent system. The report identified different agent types, including model-based agents, simple reflex agents, goal-based agents, utility-based agents, and human agents. The Guidance, Navigation, and Control system was modeled as a model-based agent, while the Flight Control System was treated as a simple reflex agent.

This literature helped connect artificial intelligence theory to a real-world engineering system. The assignment showed that intelligence in complex systems can emerge from coordination between multiple agents rather than a single centralized system. It also highlighted the importance of human-in-the-loop decision-making, redundancy, and fault recovery.

## AI and Society: Ethical Impact Analysis

The AI and Society assignment involved reviewing current literature on the ethical and societal impact of artificial intelligence. The report examined privacy and surveillance, deepfakes and misinformation, healthcare, and employment. It used ethical frameworks such as consequentialism, deontological ethics, and virtue ethics to evaluate these issues.

The sources reviewed in this project included organizations and publications such as UNESCO, the European Commission, the Electronic Frontier Foundation, the World Health Organization, the International Monetary Fund, the World Economic Forum, the OECD, and academic or technical publications on AI ethics and labor impacts.

This assignment connected directly to the course outcomes because it required reviewing and discussing current AI literature. It also helped me understand that artificial intelligence cannot be evaluated only by technical performance. Responsible AI requires attention to privacy, fairness, accountability, transparency, and human well-being.

The most important lesson from reviewing these materials was that AI systems must be evaluated from multiple perspectives. It is not enough for an AI system to produce an answer; the system should also be efficient, reliable, explainable, ethical, and appropriate for the problem being solved.
