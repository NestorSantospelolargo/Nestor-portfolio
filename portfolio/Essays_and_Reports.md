## Is Artificial Intelligence True Intelligence?

This essay explored one of the most fundamental philosophical questions in artificial intelligence: whether AI should be considered genuine intelligence or merely a simulation of intelligent behavior.

The project required analyzing and defending two competing positions. Position A argued that AI should be considered true intelligence because intelligence can be defined functionally through learning, adaptation, reasoning, and problem solving. Position B argued that AI is not true intelligence because it lacks understanding, consciousness, and intentionality.

The essay examined ideas from functionalism, Bandura's Social Learning Theory, the AI Effect, Searle's Chinese Room argument, Chalmers' work on consciousness, and Dennett's concept of intentionality. These perspectives highlighted the complexity of defining intelligence and evaluating whether artificial systems truly possess it.

### Reflection

This assignment had a significant impact on my understanding of artificial intelligence. Initially, I was more aligned with the position that AI is not true intelligence. However, researching both perspectives forced me to examine my assumptions about what intelligence actually means. The assignment helped me understand that the debate is not simply about what AI can do, but about how intelligence itself should be defined. It also prepared me for later discussions involving Large Language Models, reasoning systems, and hybrid AI architectures.
