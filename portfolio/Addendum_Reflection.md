# Portfolio Reflection Addendum

At the beginning of this course, my understanding of artificial intelligence was heavily influenced by modern AI systems such as ChatGPT and other Large Language Models (LLMs). I viewed AI primarily as a technology capable of generating responses and solving problems through natural language interaction. As the semester progressed, however, my perspective changed significantly.

One of the most important lessons I learned was that LLMs are not truly reasoning systems in the same way humans reason. While they often produce fluent, convincing, and contextually appropriate responses, they do not verify facts or logically prove their conclusions. Studying LLMs revealed that they are fundamentally prediction systems that generate text based on patterns learned from large datasets. Because of this, they can sometimes produce answers that sound correct while containing factual errors, missing constraints, or fabricated information.

As I learned more about classical AI methods, I began to appreciate the strengths of approaches that do not rely on language generation. Search algorithms, rational agents, and constraint satisfaction techniques provide mechanisms for exploring possible solutions systematically and verifying correctness. Unlike an LLM, a search algorithm can prove that a path exists or determine an optimal sequence of actions under specific assumptions. Constraint Satisfaction Problems demonstrated how logical constraints can eliminate impossible solutions and guarantee consistency. Adversarial search showed how AI systems can reason strategically about future outcomes rather than simply predicting text.

This course also changed my understanding of why hybrid AI systems are becoming increasingly important. Before taking the course, I assumed that increasingly powerful language models would eventually solve most AI problems on their own. However, the course demonstrated that combining LLMs with structured reasoning systems is what creates more reliable and trustworthy solutions. LLMs excel at interpretation of natural language, and generating human-friendly explanations. Classical AI methods excel at verification, planning, optimization, and logical consistency. By combining these approaches, hybrid systems can use the strengths of both while reducing their weaknesses.

Overall, this course upgraded my understanding of artificial intelligence from viewing AI as primarily language generation to understanding it as a broad field composed of many complementary techniques. I learned that intelligence in artificial systems is not simply the ability to produce convincing responses but also the ability to reason, plan, search, verify, and act effectively within constraints. As AI continues to evolve, I believe hybrid systems that combine language models with structured reasoning and validation will play a critical role in creating more trustworthy and effective intelligent systems.
